\subsection*{Appearance}
\addcontentsline{toc}{subsection}{Appearance}

\begin{itemize}
\item Clarity may be good to brilliant.
\begin{itemize}
\item Brilliant or Crystal Clear: gem or lens-like, more highly regarded in most styles
\item Good clarity: free from visible particulates and haze, appearing clear and transparent
\item Fair clarity: may have fine suspended particles, with a light haze or cloudy appearance
\item Poor clarity: noticeable suspended particles, cloudiness, always undesirable.
\end{itemize}
\item Oxidation is sometimes evident as dull, brownish shades or lack of brightness and vibrancy in color. However, do not assume oxidation by color alone.
\item Stronger or sweeter examples may show evidence of legs or tears on the glass.
\item Carbonation can vary from completely still to Champagne-like. At higher carbonation levels, the mousse (head) may be retained for a short time. However, gushing, foaming, and difficult-to-manage heads are faults.
\begin{itemize}
\item Still: Very light to no carbonation, but does not need to be completely flat.
\item Pétillant: Light to moderate carbonation, noticeable bubbles. Lightly sparkling, gentle effervescence, softer fizz.
\item Sparkling: Highly carbonated, often effervescent.
\end{itemize}
\end{itemize}
